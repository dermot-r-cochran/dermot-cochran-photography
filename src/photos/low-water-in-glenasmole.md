The two reservoirs in this valley were built between 1883 and 1887 for the
Rathmines Commission, and drinking water was only half the reason. The lower
one exists to guarantee a steady flow down the Dodder for the mills strung
along it — forty-five of them at the time, grinding flour, making paper and
paint and cardboard, tanning, brewing and curing bacon. They were not fed the
same water either. The stony, peat-free ground on the sides of the glen drained
into the upper reservoir, which was for drinking; the bog higher up drained
into the lower, which only had to keep the mills supplied and had no need to be
clear. The mills are long gone. The obligation to keep the river running is
what shaped the valley.
